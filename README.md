# The King and The Golden Stag

`Many` `years` ago, a *large* `herd` of `deer` **lived** in a `forest`. The `king` of the `land` **was** *fond* of `hunting`, and *every* `day` he **hunted** and **killed** a *few* `deer`. *One* `day`, the `herd` **got** together to **think** of a `way` to **stop** the *unnecessary* `killing`. They **decided** that *each* `day` *one* `deer` **would** **present** itself for the `hunt` while the `others` **hid** themselves. The `king` **would** then **chase** and **kill** *only* *one* `deer`. *Every* `evening` they **would** **draw** `lots` to **choose** the `deer` to **be** **hunted**. In *this* `way`, *every* `deer` **had** a `chance` to **be** *safe* or to **be** **killed**.

The *next* `morning` they **put** *their* `plan` to `work`. When the `king` and *his* `courtiers` **came** to the `forest`, they **found** *only* *one* `deer` near a `stream`. They **chased** and **killed** it. When they **looked** for *more* `deer`, they **could** not **find** *another*. At *last* they **went** back to the `palace`.

The *second* `day` the *same* `thing` **occurred**, and also on the *third* `day`. When it **happened** again on the *fourth* `day`, the `king` **understood** what the `deer` **were** **trying** to **say**. He **stopped** **looking** for the `deer` that **were** **hiding** and *only* **hunted** the *one* that **was** near the `stream`.

A *few* `weeks` **passed** and the `deer's` `plan` **was** **working** well. *One* `evening` the *golden* `stag` **was** **picked** in the `lottery`. He **was** the *chief* `chief` of the `deer` and *his* `herd` **loved** him. They **were** *sad* that he **had** to **die** and *some* of them **offered** to **take** *his* `place` but he **refused**, it **was** *only* *fair* that he **should** **take** *his* `turn` like the `others`.

The *next* `morning`, the `king` **saw** the *golden* `stag` **standing** beside the `stream`. He **had** often **seen** the *beautiful* `stag` but he **had** never **tried** to **kill** it. "I **shall** not **harm** *such* a *wonderful* `beast`," the `king` **told** *his* `courtiers`. *That* `day` there **was** no `hunting`. The *golden* `stag's` `life` **was** **saved**.

A *few* `evenings` *later* a `doe` **came** to the *golden* `stag` in `tears`. With *her* **was** *her* *little* `fawn`. She **had** **been** **chosen** for the `hunt` the *following* `day`. "If I **am** **killed** tomorrow," she **told** the *golden* `stag` *sadly*, "*my* `fawn` **will** **die** too. He **is** too *young* to **eat** `leaves` and `grass`, and *no one* else **will** **give** him `milk`." She **begged** the *golden* `stag` to **send** *another* instead. "I **promise** to **take** *my* `turn` as soon as *my* `fawn` **is** *old* enough to **look** after himself."

The *golden* `stag` **felt** *sorry* for the `doe`. "I **will** **make** *some* *other* `arrangements`," he **promised** her. "**Take** *your* `fawn` to a *safe* `place` till he **is** *old* enough."

The *next* `morning`, the *golden* `stag` **took** the `doe's` `place` at the `stream`. When the `king` **saw** the *golden* `stag`, he **became** *angry*. "The `deer` **are** **trying** to **trick** me," he **shouted**. "Today I **will** not **spare** the `life` of the *golden* `stag`, however *beautiful* he **is**."

A `sage` who **lived** in the `forest` **had** **seen** the `meeting` between the *golden* `stag` and the `doe`. He **came** forward and **told** the `king`, "You **are** *wrong* to **doubt** the *golden* `stag`. Yesterday, a `doe` **came** to him with *her* *young* `fawn`. It **was** *her* `turn` to **be** **hunted** today. If she **died**, so **would** *her* *little* *helpless* `fawn`. *This* *golden* `stag` **promised** the `doe` *her* `safety`. As he **could** not **ask** *another* `deer` to **take** *her* `place`, he **has** **come** himself. He **is** indeed a *true* `king`, **showing** *great* `care` for *his* `herd`."

When the `king` **heard** *these* `words`, he **was** *ashamed*. The *golden* `stag` **had** **put** a *little* `fawn's` `welfare` above *his* *own* `life`. While he **had** **cared** *only* for *his* *own* `pleasure` and **killed** so *many* `deer`. The `king` **vowed** never to **hunt** again as long as he **lived**.
